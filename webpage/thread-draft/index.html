Jianshu Zhang @your_handle1/11 · Main post260/280

Can a VLM watch a stream of videos and build its own library of reusable skills, deciding when to reuse a skill and when to create a new one?

We introduce Video2Skill and a new problem: Streaming Embodied Skill Discovery (SESD). 🧵

📄 arxiv.org/abs/2609.36691

Alt text

Animated loop: robot videos stream in; each event is tagged NEW (orange) and flies into a skill library, or REUSE (teal) and links to an existing skill; the library grows from grasp, transfer, place to pour.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle2/11 · Why skills275/280

Why skills? Manipulation varies across objects and scenes, yet shares a small set of reusable skills, and planning with them helps agents generalize.

But an agent can only plan with skills it knows. Recovering skills from experience, the inverse of planning, grows that set.

Alt text

Two new tasks: "put the cup in the sink" is planned as grasp, transfer, place; "water the plant" fails because the pour skill is unknown.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle3/11 · The task270/280

SESD: a model watches videos one after another. For each manipulation it
1) locates it in time,
2) writes it as a skill call, e.g. grasp(obj, src),
3) decides: reuse a skill from its library, or create a new one.

The library persists, so every decision shapes the next.

Alt text

Streaming Embodied Skill Discovery: a robot video with events tagged REUSE and NEW, next to a persistent skill library with usage counts.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle4/11 · Benchmark258/280

Video2Skill spans robot tabletop manipulation (DROID/RH20T via RoboInter) and egocentric kitchens (HD-EPIC): 12,429 verified skill calls, 48 transformation classes.

Scoring is naming-invariant: we check how events are grouped, not what the model calls them.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle5/11 · Finding 1241/280

Finding 1: across 19 open-source VLMs, many models group events near chance (best ARI ≤ 27 out of 100).

Scale is not a reliable fix: going from Qwen3.5-4B to 27B raises ARI on HD-EPIC (8.7 → 19.9) but lowers it on RoboInter (19.4 → 4.6).

Alt text

Best skill-grouping agreement (ARI) of 19 open-source VLMs is at most 27 out of 100; scaling Qwen3.5 from 4B to 27B raises ARI on HD-EPIC (8.7 to 19.9) but lowers it on RoboInter (19.4 to 4.6).

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle6/11 · Finding 2269/280

Finding 2: how models fail depends on how perception and library updates are coupled.

• Joint models (video + library in one pass) merge distinct transformations: pouring and placing become one skill.
• Models updating the library from text duplicate recurring ones.

Alt text

Joint models merge distinct transformations (pour absorbed into place); text-update models duplicate recurring ones (grasp re-created as pick_up, grab, take).

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle7/11 · Finding 3264/280

Finding 3: fine-tuning, incl. our counterfactual library-state rebalancing (CLaRe), improves grouping but exposes a deeper bottleneck.

Models consolidate familiar skills yet rarely expand the library: on HD-EPIC it stalls at 21–22 skills vs. 46 reference classes.

Alt text

After fine-tuning the learned library holds 21-22 skills versus 46 reference transformation classes.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle8/11 · Finding 3, cont.228/280

Transformations never seen in training (e.g. grating, shredding) are located in time even more often than seen ones, but almost never get a new skill: at most 1 of the 9–10 covered unseen classes is named as new when first seen.

Alt text

A lemon-grating clip, a transformation unseen in training: located in time (coverage 58-66%), but at most 1 of 9-10 covered unseen classes gets a new skill.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle9/11 · Takeaway and scope267/280

Takeaway: describing single events is not the bottleneck. Recognizing when your existing skills are not enough is.

To be clear about scope: our skills are semantic abstractions of observed transformations, not executable controllers, and we cover two domains so far.

Alt text

The central challenge: recognizing when existing skills are insufficient.
