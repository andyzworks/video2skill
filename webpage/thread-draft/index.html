Can a VLM watch a stream of videos and build its own library of reusable skills, deciding when to reuse a skill and when to create a new one?

We introduce Video2Skill and a new problem: Streaming Embodied Skill Discovery (SESD). 🧵

📄 arxiv.org/abs/2609.36691

Alt text

Animated loop: robot videos stream in; each event is tagged NEW (orange) and flies into a skill library, or REUSE (teal) and links to an existing skill; the library grows from grasp, transfer, place to pour.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle2/6 · Task and benchmark681 chars · folds

The task: watch videos one after another. For each manipulation, locate it in time, write it as a skill call like grasp(obj, src), and decide: reuse a known skill or create a new one.

Video2Skill covers robot (DROID/RH20T) and kitchen (HD-EPIC) video: 12,429 verified calls.…
Why skills: manipulation varies across objects and scenes but shares a small set of reusable skills, and planning with them helps agents generalize. An agent can only plan with skills it knows, so recovering skills from experience is the inverse of planning.

Scoring is naming-invariant: 48 reference classes are never shown to the model, and we compare how events are grouped, not what they are called.

Alt text

Streaming Embodied Skill Discovery: a robot video with events tagged REUSE and NEW, next to a persistent skill library with usage counts.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle3/6 · Finding 1241/280

Finding 1: across 19 open-source VLMs, many models group events near chance (best ARI ≤ 27 out of 100).

Scale is not a reliable fix: going from Qwen3.5-4B to 27B raises ARI on HD-EPIC (8.7 → 19.9) but lowers it on RoboInter (19.4 → 4.6).

Alt text

Best skill-grouping agreement (ARI) of 19 open-source VLMs is at most 27 out of 100; scaling Qwen3.5 from 4B to 27B raises ARI on HD-EPIC (8.7 to 19.9) but lowers it on RoboInter (19.4 to 4.6).

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle4/6 · Finding 2269/280

Finding 2: how models fail depends on how perception and library updates are coupled.

• Joint models (video + library in one pass) merge distinct transformations: pouring and placing become one skill.
• Models updating the library from text duplicate recurring ones.

Alt text

Joint models merge distinct transformations (pour absorbed into place); text-update models duplicate recurring ones (grasp re-created as pick_up, grab, take).

Finding 3: fine-tuning (incl. our CLaRe) improves grouping but exposes a deeper bottleneck: familiar skills get consolidated, yet the library rarely grows.

On HD-EPIC it stalls at 21–22 skills vs. 46 classes. Unseen transformations are located but almost never named as new.…
For the 11 HD-EPIC classes absent from training, coverage after fine-tuning is 58–66% (higher than for seen classes), yet at most 1 of the 9–10 covered unseen classes gets a new skill name when first seen.

Left: after fine-tuning the learned library holds 21-22 skills versus 46 reference classes. Right: a lemon-grating clip, a transformation unseen in training, is located in time (coverage 58-66%) but at most 1 of 9-10 such classes is named as new.
