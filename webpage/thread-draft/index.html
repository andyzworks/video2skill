Manipulation behaviors vary widely, but share a small set of reusable skills: wiping a table or a window is one skill. Planning with skills is how embodied agents generalize.

But agents can only plan with skills they know. Can VLMs discover them by watching?

🧵 Video2Skill…
Recovering skills from experience is the inverse of planning: it grows an agent's repertoire over time and turns raw video into skill-annotated data for training future agents.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle2/7 · The task450 chars · folds

The task: a model watches videos one after another. Each time something is manipulated, it has to find when it happens, write it down as a skill like grasp(object, from), and decide: is this a skill it already has, or a new one?

Its skill library carries over from video to… video, so early choices shape later ones.

Our benchmark covers robot tabletop (DROID/RH20T) and kitchen (HD-EPIC) video: 12,429 verified skill calls over 48 kinds of actions.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle3/7 · How we measure it789 chars · folds

How we score it: three questions, each with its own numbers.

1) Did it find the actions?
• Coverage: share of real actions the model locates in time.

2) Did it group them right? Names don't matter, only which actions end up in the same skill. We check every pair of actions:…
• Pair precision: of pairs it put in one skill, how many are truly the same action. Low = lumping.
• Pair recall: of pairs that are truly the same action, how many it put together. Low = splitting.
• ARI: one overall grouping score. 100 = perfect, 0 = random.

3) Did it create and reuse at the right time?
• Create recall: when an action appears for the first time, how often it adds a new skill.
• Reuse recall: when an action appears again, how often it uses an existing one.

Finding 1: today's VLMs can't do this out of the box. Across 19 open models, the best grouping score (ARI) is 27 out of 100, where 0 is random. Bigger models don't reliably help.

They fail in two opposite ways. Models that watch the video and manage the library together (joint… models) lump different actions into one skill: low pair precision. Models that first describe the video in text keep inventing new names for the same action: low pair recall.

Alt text

Animated comparison: a joint model files pour under place, lumping different actions together; a text-based model creates grasp, pick_up and grab for the same action. 19 open VLMs; best grouping score (ARI) at most 27 of 100; bigger models do not reliably help.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle5/7 · Finding 2935 chars · folds

Finding 2: so we designed three ways to fine-tune them. They differ in which library the model sees while practicing. No single one wins on every score; each has its own strength (counted over 8 settings: 2 models × 2 designs × 2 datasets).

A) Oracle-history SFT: practice on… the correct library, replayed video by video. Best at keeping different actions apart: top pair precision in 5 of 8, top ARI for joint models in 4 of 4.

B) Counterfactual Library-state Rebalancing (CLaRe): same video, edited library. Remove the skill it needs and it must create one; add a similar-looking but different skill and its answer shouldn't change. Finds the most actions: top coverage in 3 of 4 joint settings.

C) On-policy correction: practice on the library the model built itself, mistakes included, with a teacher labeling the right move. Best at keeping repeats of one action together: top pair recall in 5 of 8, top reuse recall in 7 of 8.

Alt text

Animated explainer of three fine-tuning strategies, each with its own strength. A, Oracle-history SFT: practice on the correct library; keeps different actions apart. B, Counterfactual Library-state Rebalancing (CLaRe): same video, edited library, so removing a needed skill forces a new one and adding a look-alike changes nothing; finds the most actions. C, On-policy correction: practice on the model's own library, mistakes included; keeps repeats together. A grid counts settings where each is best: coverage 0/3/1 of 4; pair precision 5/2/1; pair recall 1/2/5; ARI 4/2/2; create recall 6/3/2; reuse recall 0/2/7 of 8. No single strategy wins everywhere.

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle6/7 · Finding 3584 chars · folds

Finding 3: training fixes a lot, so what's left? One specific decision: when to add a new skill.

Say grating shows up for the first time. The right move is a new skill. Trained joint models usually file it under a skill they already have.

• Repeats: 97–99% go to an existing… skill
• First sightings: only 19–40% get a new one
• They do see these actions: ones never seen in training are found in time 58–66% of the time (more often than familiar ones), yet only 0–1 of ~10 get their own skill
• On kitchen video the library stops at 21–22 skills while 46 kinds of actions appear

Jianshu Zhang @your_handle7/7 · Takeaway and links882 chars · folds

Takeaway: matching an action to a skill you already have is learnable: joint models' ARI goes 19 → 60 with training. Noticing that none fits is much harder. Even with CLaRe, which trains exactly this decision, joint models add a new skill for at most 40% of first sightings.…
That decision is how an agent's repertoire grows from what it sees. A single overall score hides it, so we measure it on its own, and we think it's the right target for what comes next.
